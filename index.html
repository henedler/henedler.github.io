Research

I'm a radio astronomer and currently a Bell Burnell Fellow at ASTRON in the Netherlands. My main research interests are the following:

Galaxy evolution

I study how the galaxies we observe in our Universe evolved, and how they interact with their surroundings. Radio observations are central to understanding the processes that shape galaxies: they trace the magnetic fields and energetic particles in and around galaxies, which regulate their star formation and gas supply. In particular, I am interested in how the star-formation process is connected to the radio emission of galaxies, and how ram pressure stripping transforms cluster galaxies. Low-frequency radio observations are an excellent probe of ongoing ram pressure stripping, since they allow us to detect the radio tails of stripped non-thermal plasma (see image).

Radio signatures of exoplanetary systems

Exoplanets can be sources of low-frequency radio emission, providing an independent radio-route for exoplanet detection, and allowing us to infer the magnetic fields of exoplanets. I use circular polarization images from low-frequency observations to hunt these exotic radio signals. One promising candidate for magnetic star-planet interaction is GJ725A, for which I detected sub-50 MHz, highly circularly polarized transient emission (Edler et al., in rev.). I am currently following up this system with monitoring campaigns.

Radio surveys

Low-frequency radio surveys are the best way to discover old cosmic-ray plasma in winds and tails of star-froming galaxies and in the ancient lobes of past phases of AGN activity, which become invisible at higher frequency. They also allow us to search for exotic radio sources, such as coherent emission from stars and exoplanets, as well as extreme pulsars. I am involved in carrying out the LOFAR LBA Sky Survey (LoLSS), the most sensitive survey of the sky below 100 MHz, and I lead the ViCTORIA LOFAR HBA survey, the most sensitive radio survey of the Virgo Cluster to date.

Radio interferometry techniques

I also work on extending the observational capabilities at radio wavelengths, in particular at low frequencies. How can we better correct for the Earth's ionosphere, which corrupts low-frequency observations? How can we increase image fidelity and resolution? A lot of my work focuses on LOFAR 2.0, where I co-develop the standard calibration pipeline for the low band (LiLF), and where I am developing the usage of the very long baseline interferometry (VLBI) mode (see image). Looking ahead, I want to carry the lessons learned from LOFAR over to the calibration and data challenges of SKA-Low.
