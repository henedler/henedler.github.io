I'm a radio astronomer and currently a Bell Burnell Fellow at ASTRON in the Netherlands. I am interested in how the galaxies we observe in our Universe evolved, and how they interact with their surroundings. Radio observations are central to understanding the processes that shape galaxies: they trace the magnetic fields and energetic particles in and around galaxies, which regulate their star formation and gas supply.

I also work on extending the observational capabilities at radio wavelengths, in particular at low frequencies. How can we better correct for the Earth's ionosphere, which corrupts low-frequency observations? How can we increase image fidelity and resolution? Part of my work focuses on the LOFAR Low Band, where I co-develop the standard calibration pipeline LiLF, and contribute to the LOFAR LBA Sky Survey LoLSS. Looking ahead, I want to carry the lessons learned from LOFAR over to the calibration and data challenges of SKA-Low.

Research

Ram-pressure stripping

  • LOFAR HBA 144 MHz
  • LOFAR LBA 54 MHz
  • MeerKAT 1.3 GHz

When galaxies fall into a cluster, the hot intracluster medium pushes against them like a headwind and strips their gas. In the radio we see the cosmic rays and magnetic fields that are swept out with it, as long tails behind the galaxies. I study this process in the Virgo cluster as part of the ViCTORIA project, and in Abell 1367, where a shock in the cluster gas has produced the longest known tails of this kind.

Edler et al. 2024, A&A 683, A149 · Edler et al. 2026, A&A 706, A240

Radio galaxies and the intracluster medium

  • LOFAR LBA 54 MHz
  • LOFAR HBA 144 MHz
  • MeerKAT 1.3 GHz

The plasma launched by active galactic nuclei keeps interacting with the cluster around it long after the jets switch off. It is bent by the motion of its host, mixed into the cluster gas, and can be re-energised by turbulence and shocks. The lowest radio frequencies trace this oldest plasma best. I study these interactions in Abell 1033, Abell 1367, the merging Virgo subcluster around M49, and in M87 itself.

Edler et al. 2022, A&A 666, A3 · Spasic, Edler et al. 2024, A&A 690, A195

Low-frequency techniques

  • LOFAR LBA 30–80 MHz
  • LOFAR 2.0
  • SKA-Low

Below 100 MHz, the Earth's ionosphere distorts the incoming radio waves, and correcting for it is the main obstacle to sharp images. I co-develop LiLF, the standard calibration pipeline for the LOFAR Low Band, contribute to the LOFAR LBA Sky Survey, and lead the development of wide-field VLBI with the LOFAR Low Band, which brings sub-arcsecond resolution to these frequencies. The same capabilities open new windows on nearby stars: we detected coherent 45 MHz emission from the planet-hosting star GJ 725A.

de Gasperin, Edler et al. 2023, A&A 673, A165 · Edler et al. 2021, A&A 652, A37 · LiLF on GitHub
